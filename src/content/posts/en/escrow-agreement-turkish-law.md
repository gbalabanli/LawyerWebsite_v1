Drafting an effective **escrow agreement in Turkey** requires careful attention to local legal frameworks, particularly when foreign parties, cross-border payments, or high-value assets are involved. Whether you are closing a commercial transaction, transferring shares, or securing real estate funds, understanding how these agreements operate under Turkish law is essential to protect your interests. This article explains the legal nature of escrow arrangements in Turkey, provides a clause-by-clause drafting checklist, and outlines key enforceability considerations.

Under Turkish law, escrow arrangements do not have a single dedicated statute bearing the exact title "escrow." Instead, they are typically structured as **sui generis (unique) or mixed contracts** under the **Turkish Code of Obligations**. Because Turkish law permits contractual freedom within statutory boundaries, parties can tailor these agreements to deposit funds, documents, or shares with a neutral third party. ## Legal Nature of Escrow Agreements Under Turkish Law

Because an escrow arrangement involves placing assets in the hands of a neutral intermediary until specific conditions are met, it often combines elements of agency, safekeeping, and conditional obligations. The **Turkish Code of Obligations** governs these underlying legal concepts. 

The primary challenge in drafting an escrow agreement in Turkey lies in balancing the control retained by the principal parties with the independent duties of the intermediary. If an agreement lacks clear mechanical triggers, a dispute can lead to prolonged court intervention. ## Essential Clauses Checklist for an Escrow Agreement in Turkey

To ensure enforceability and minimize ambiguity, every well-drafted escrow contract governed by Turkish law should incorporate specific structural clauses. Below is a checklist of critical sections to include during the drafting process:

*   **Identification of Parties and the Escrow Agent:** Clearly define the buyer (depositor), the seller (beneficiary), and the neutral trustee. Specify whether the trustee is an independent corporate entity, a bank, or a qualified legal professional.
*   **Deposit and Description of Escrowed Assets:** Explicitly state what is being deposited—whether it is fiat currency (and in which bank account), share certificates, title deed documents, or intellectual property codes.
*   **Release Conditions and Milestones:** Detail the exact, objective conditions required for the release of the assets. Avoid vague terminology; instead, use verifiable documentation such as official registry transcripts, signed delivery receipts, or court-certified notices.
*   **Trustee Duties, Fees, and Liability:** Define the standard of care expected from the trustee, the exact fee structure for their services, and limitations on their liability in case of third-party disruptions or system failures.
*   **Dispute Resolution and Governing Law:** Establish clear pathways for what happens if the parties disagree on whether release conditions have been fulfilled.

## Defining Release Conditions and Mechanics

The core purpose of any escrow agreement in Turkey is the release mechanism. Vague release triggers are the leading cause of failed enforcement. A robust contract must outline a step-by-step procedure for what happens when a milestone is reached.

For example, if the agreement secures a payment pending a regulatory approval, the contract should state that the trustee will release the funds only upon presentation of an official, stamped approval document from the relevant Turkish governmental authority. It should also establish a strict timeline for objections. If one party raises a good-faith objection within the stipulated review window, the trustee must continue holding the assets until the dispute is resolved by mutual written amendment or judicial intervention.

## Trustee Duties, Standard of Care, and Liability

The escrow agent acts as a fiduciary. Under Turkish legal principles, a professional trustee is expected to exercise the care and diligence of a prudent merchant or professional. 

The agreement must explicitly limit the trustee's discretion. A trustee should not be permitted to interpret ambiguous commercial intentions; their role must remain strictly administrative. Additionally, the contract should outline how the trustee handles interest accrued on deposited funds, who bears banking fees, and the exact indemnification procedures if the trustee is dragged into a dispute between the buyer and seller through no fault of their own.

## Dispute Resolution and Enforceability in Turkish Courts

Even with the best drafting, disputes can arise. When an escrow agreement in Turkey leads to litigation, enforcement depends heavily on how clearly the obligations are stated. Turkish courts and execution offices rely on literal interpretations of contract terms.

Parties frequently include an arbitration clause or designate specific courts—such as the commercial courts of Istanbul or Ankara—to handle disputes swiftly. It is important to note that interim injunctions (*ihtiyati tedbir*) may be sought if there is an imminent risk that a party will wrongfully attempt to withdraw or dissipate escrowed assets. However, whether an injunction is granted depends entirely on the specific facts and documentation presented to the judge.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are escrow agreements legally recognized in Turkey?
Yes, escrow agreements are legally recognized as mixed or sui generis contracts under the Turkish Code of Obligations, provided they do not violate mandatory statutory rules, public order, or morality.

### What happens if the escrow trustee breaches their duty in Turkey?
If a trustee breaches their contractual obligations or fails to act with professional diligence, they can be held liable for damages under Turkish contract and tort law, depending on the specific agreement terms.

### Can an escrow agreement in Turkey be governed by foreign law?
While parties to cross-border transactions often choose foreign governing law, enforcement before Turkish courts or execution offices for assets located in Turkey typically requires careful compliance with Turkish public policy and mandatory rules.
