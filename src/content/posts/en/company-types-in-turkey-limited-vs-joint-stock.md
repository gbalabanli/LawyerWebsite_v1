Choosing the right legal form is one of the most critical decisions for foreign founders launching a business. When evaluating the primary types of companies in Turkey, most international investors focus on two main corporate structures: the Limited Liability Company (Limited Şirket) and the Joint-Stock Company (Anonim Şirket). Understanding how these entities differ regarding liability, governance, capital requirements, and share transfers helps ensure your enterprise starts on a solid legal foundation. ## Main Types of Companies in Turkey for Foreign Founders

Under the Turkish Commercial Code, commercial entities take several forms, but foreign founders almost exclusively choose between the Limited Liability Company (LLC) and the Joint-Stock Company (JSC). Both provide distinct advantages depending on your operational goals, scale, and funding strategies. 

An LLC is typically favored by small to medium-sized businesses, e-commerce ventures, and agency models due to its simpler corporate governance and lower administrative overhead. Conversely, a JSC is designed for larger enterprises, tech startups seeking venture capital, and businesses planning to raise public funds or issue bonds. In practice, minimum capital thresholds are established by law and revised periodically; you should always confirm current amounts with legal counsel before filing your incorporation paperwork. Generally, joint-stock companies require a higher baseline capital commitment than limited liability companies.

Furthermore, business trajectories change. Many foreign-owned businesses start as a limited company for operational simplicity and later convert into a joint-stock company. Founders often choose this structural conversion when they prepare for institutional investment, complex employee stock option pools, or public offerings, as JSCs offer a more flexible framework for equity financing and large-scale share management.

## Comparing Limited Liability Companies and Joint-Stock Companies

To choose the correct vehicle among the available types of companies in Turkey, investors must compare their structural mechanics across several core areas:

* **Shareholders and Ownership:** An LLC can be formed by one or more real persons or legal entities. A JSC can also be established by a single shareholder (individual or corporate) and allows for an unrestricted number of shareholders.
* **Liability and Public Debts:** In both structures, shareholders generally enjoy limited liability restricted to their subscribed capital contributions. However, founders must note important liability exceptions regarding public debts. Under Turkish law, if a company fails to pay its public liabilities—such as corporate taxes or social security premium debts—and the company assets are insufficient, directors or certain shareholders may face secondary liability for these unpaid public obligations depending on their management role and share status.
* **Share Transfers:** Transferring shares in an LLC requires a formal notarial contract, the approval of the general assembly, and registration in the trade registry, making the process more formal and controlled. In contrast, transferring shares in a JSC is significantly easier, usually requiring simple endorsement of share certificates or book-entry transfers without mandatory notary intervention, unless restricted by the articles of association or a [Shareholder Agreement for Turkish Companies](/en/blog/shareholder-agreement-turkey-foreign-owned-companies).
* **Governance and Management:** An LLC is managed by one or more directors (managers), at least one of whom must typically be appointed with management authority. A JSC is managed by a Board of Directors, which must consist of at least one member (individual or legal entity), making it more suitable for corporate governance models involving diverse stakeholders.

## Alternative Legal Structures and Representative Offices

While LLCs and JSCs dominate foreign direct investment, Turkish law provides other structures that may suit specific niche activities or preparatory phases:

* **Sole Proprietorship (Şahıs Şirketi):** Suitable for individual freelancers or small-scale service providers. Setting up is fast and inexpensive, but owners face personal, unlimited liability for all business debts, and tax brackets are progressive.
* **Branches (Şube):** Foreign parent companies can establish a branch office in Turkey. A branch is legally tied to the foreign headquarters, meaning the parent company retains full legal and financial liability for the branch's local operations.
* **Lieu / Liaison Offices (İrtibat Bürosu):** Designed strictly for market research, promotional activities, and liaison work. Liaison offices are legally prohibited from engaging in any commercial activity, generating revenue, or issuing invoices in Turkey.
* **Collective and Commandite Companies:** These traditional partnership structures involve unlimited or mixed personal liability for partners and are rarely utilized by foreign investors today.

## Decision Checklist for Choosing Your Entity

Deciding between the various types of companies in Turkey depends on your specific business model, capital planning, and long-term exit strategy. Consider the following checklist items when making your final determination:

1. **Capital Availability:** Can your business meet the higher baseline capital expectations of a joint-stock company, or is a limited liability company a better financial fit for launch?
2. **Future Fundraising Plans:** Do you intend to bring in outside venture capital, angel investors, or issue complex stock classes? If yes, a joint-stock company provides a much smoother framework.
3. **Share Transfer Frequency:** Do you anticipate frequent trading or transfers of ownership shares? Joint-stocks offer streamlined transfer mechanisms compared to notarial requirements for limited companies.
4. **Administrative Budget:** Are you prepared for the mandatory board structures and auditing requirements that frequently accompany larger joint-stock operations?
5. **Tax and Compliance Support:** Have you consulted local professionals to evaluate ongoing accounting, tax filing, and compliance obligations for your chosen entity?

Every business scenario carries unique risks and operational requirements. This overview provides general guidance, but your final choice depends on the specific facts of your venture.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a limited company and a joint-stock company in Turkey?
The primary differences involve minimum capital requirements, share transfer procedures, management structures, and suitability for outside investment or public offerings. Limited companies suit smaller, closely held businesses, while joint-stock companies accommodate larger enterprises and outside investors.

### Can a foreign national own 100% of a company in Turkey?
Yes, foreign individuals and corporate entities can hold 100% of the shares in both limited liability companies and joint-stock companies under Turkish commercial law, with very few sector-specific restrictions.

### Are shareholders fully protected from corporate debts?
Shareholders generally face liability limited to their contributed capital. However, statutory exceptions exist regarding unpaid public debts such as taxes and social security contributions, where directors or designated managers may face secondary collection procedures if company assets fall short.

### Can a limited company later be converted into a joint-stock company?
Yes, Turkish commercial legislation permits structural conversions. A growing limited company can transform into a joint-stock company through a formal statutory conversion process when business needs, equity financing, or shareholder requirements dictate the change.
