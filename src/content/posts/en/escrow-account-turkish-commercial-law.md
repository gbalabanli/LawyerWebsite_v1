Navigating cross-border transactions and high-value domestic deals often requires secure mechanisms to protect both buyers and sellers. Utilizing an escrow account Turkish commercial law framework helps mitigate counterparty risk by ensuring that capital moves only when exact contractual milestones are met. This guide breaks down how these accounts operate, the compliance steps required by local regulators, and what foreign or local businesses must look out for.

Commercial parties frequently confuse a true escrow setup with standard blocked bank accounts or pledges. Understanding these foundational distinctions before approaching a financial institution is essential for smooth transaction execution.

## True Escrow Versus Blocked Bank Accounts

Under Turkish practice, business owners often default to asking banks for a standard "blocked account" (*bloke hesap*) or a commercial pledge. However, a true escrow arrangement differs fundamentally in its tripartite nature. While a blocked account typically places a unilateral hold on funds at the request of one account holder, a genuine escrow account involves an independent escrow agent—often a commercial bank or authorized financial institution—holding funds pursuant to strict instructions agreed upon by both the payer and the payee. 

Funds in a true escrow account cannot be unilaterally frozen or withdrawn by either party. Release requires joint written instructions or the objective verification of milestone documents specified in the foundational agreement. This distinction matters greatly when structuring mergers, acquisitions, or high-value asset purchases, as it dictates how disputes and fund releases are handled. ## How an Escrow Account Works Under Turkish Commercial Law

While the Turkish Commercial Code and general banking legislation do not contain a single, exhaustive chapter exclusively dedicated to escrow accounts, these arrangements are fully enforceable under the principle of contract freedom. Financial institutions structure escrow mechanisms by combining standard deposit account regulations with specialized escrow agreements (*tevdi sözleşmesi*). 

The operational lifecycle generally follows three main stages:
* **Drafting and Execution:** The buyer, seller, and the institutional escrow agent execute a multi-party agreement outlining release conditions, inspection periods, and dispute resolution venues.
* **Deposit of Funds:** The buyer deposits the purchase price or security amount into the designated segregated account.
* **Verification and Release:** Upon presentation of agreed compliance documents—such as commercial registry certificates, board resolutions, or shipping receipts—the agent disburses the funds to the seller.

For smaller digital transactions or specific real estate deals, alternative mechanisms like the official [Turkey's Secure Payment System (Güvenli Ödeme) Explained](/en/blog/secure-payment-system-turkey) may also serve as a streamlined statutory alternative.

## MASAK Compliance, AML, and KYC Obligations

Opening and operating any corporate financial structure in Turkey requires strict adherence to anti-money laundering regulations. Under Law No. 5549 on Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime, commercial banks and financial intermediaries must enforce rigorous Know Your Customer (KYC) protocols.

MASAK (Financial Crimes Investigation Board) mandates that institutions verify the identity of all corporate directors, authorized signatories, and ultimate beneficial owners holding a significant percentage of shares. When setting up an account, foreign entities must provide apostilled corporate documents, tax identification numbers, and verifiable documentation detailing the commercial rationale behind the transaction. Failure to satisfy these compliance checks will halt the account opening process immediately.

## Tax Treatment and Financial Considerations

Tax implications surrounding escrow arrangements require careful upfront planning to avoid unexpected liabilities. Depending on how the agreement is structured, several tax factors typically come into play:
* **Stamp Duty (*Damga Vergisi*):** Multi-party commercial agreements executed in Turkey often attract stamp duty calculated as a fixed percentage of the transaction or monetary limit stated in the contract.
* **Interest Withholding:** If the escrowed capital earns interest while sitting in the bank account, that interest income is generally subject to local withholding taxes.
* **Double Taxation Treaties:** Foreign entities should evaluate whether their home jurisdiction provides tax credits or exemptions for withholding taxes incurred during the holding period.

Because tax enforcement depends heavily on the specific wording of the contract and the nature of the underlying assets, consulting with a qualified tax advisor alongside legal counsel is strongly advised.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an escrow account under Turkish commercial law?
An escrow account is a specialized arrangement where funds are deposited with a neutral third party, such as a bank or authorized financial institution, and released only when predefined contractual conditions are met.

### What are the primary AML and KYC requirements for opening an escrow account in Turkey?
Under Law No. 5549 on Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime, applicants must complete thorough KYC checks enforced by MASAK, verifying ultimate beneficial ownership and the legitimate source of funds.

### How is interest and tax handled on funds held in a Turkish escrow account?
Interest earned on escrowed funds is generally subject to withholding taxes, and the underlying escrow agreement may trigger stamp duty under Turkish tax laws depending on its drafted terms.
