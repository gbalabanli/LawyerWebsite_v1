Navigating escrow in Turkish M&A transactions is critical for both domestic and foreign investors seeking to safeguard their capital against post-closing liabilities. When executing a share purchase agreement (SPA) in Turkey, buyers and sellers frequently utilize an escrow arrangement to bridge valuation gaps, secure indemnity claims, and manage regulatory conditions such as competition clearance. Understanding how to structure these clauses, define caps and baskets, and establish clear release mechanisms can prevent costly disputes. This guide examines how escrow functions in Turkish corporate transactions and provides practical insights for drafting secure SPA terms.

## Structuring Escrow Clauses in Turkish Share Purchase Agreements

Drafting robust escrow provisions requires careful alignment between the SPA and the standalone escrow agreement. In Turkish M&A practice, the escrow clause within the SPA sets out the foundational obligation to deposit a portion of the purchase price into a secure account upon closing. This deposit serves as a dedicated pool of funds for potential breach of representation and warranty claims, tax indemnities, or specific leakage items identified before the transaction.

When negotiating these clauses, parties must clearly define the exact percentage of the purchase price to be withheld. While typical transactions see escrow amounts ranging from 10% to 20%, high-risk sectors or targets with complex historical liabilities may warrant higher retentions. Furthermore, the SPA must detail the precise conditions under which claims can be brought against the escrow fund, ensuring that the seller has adequate notice and an opportunity to defend against any asserted claims.

## Release Mechanics for Escrow in Turkish M&A

Establishing predictable release mechanics is essential to prevent funds from remaining frozen indefinitely. In a standard Turkish M&A deal, the release of escrow funds is tied to the expiration of warranty survival periods, completion of post-closing audits, or the final resolution of pending claims. Parties must choose who will physically hold the funds, which can range from a commercial bank or financial institution to a specialized law firm operating through dedicated accounts, as further discussed in resources covering escrow accounts under Turkish commercial law.

The release procedure typically follows a joint instruction model. Once a specific milestone is reached—such as the passing of a 12-month or 24-month survival period without any active indemnity claims—both the buyer and seller sign a release notice instructing the agent to disburse the remaining balance to the seller. If a dispute arises over a claim, the undisputed portion of the escrow is usually released while the disputed amount remains blocked until a court judgment or arbitral award is rendered.

## Caps, Baskets, and Warranty Survival Periods

Escrow mechanisms do not operate in a vacuum; they interact directly with the liability caps, de minimis thresholds, and baskets negotiated in the SPA. A basket establishes a minimum threshold that cumulative claims must exceed before the buyer can tap into the escrow account, while a cap limits the seller's maximum aggregate liability.

In Turkish corporate deals, warranty survival periods typically mirror the escrow duration. Fundamental representations—such as title to shares, tax liabilities, and corporate capacity—often carry longer survival periods aligned with statutory limitation periods under Turkish law, whereas operational warranties expire much sooner. Properly aligning these timeframes ensures that buyers do not attempt to draw down escrow funds after the contractual liability period has lapsed.

## Managing Competition Clearance and Regulatory Timing

Cross-border M&A transactions in Turkey frequently trigger merger control filings with the Turkish Competition Authority. Because closing cannot legally occur until clearance is granted, parties often structure locked-box or completion accounts mechanisms where funds are placed into preliminary escrow pending regulatory approval.

This interim escrow phase protects the buyer against any unauthorized value extraction or ordinary course leakage by the target company between signing and closing. If the competition authority blocks the transaction or imposes overly burdensome conditions, the escrow agreement must explicitly dictate whether the funds are returned to the buyer or handled according to pre-agreed break-fee arrangements. Careful contract review is necessary to ensure these regulatory contingencies do not conflict with banking regulations or currency control rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the typical size of an escrow account in Turkish M&A transactions?
Escrow amounts typically range from 10% to 20% of the total purchase price, depending on the perceived risks identified during due diligence and the financial health of the target company.

### How long do escrow funds usually remain locked after closing?
Escrow survival periods generally range from 12 to 24 months, mirroring the standard warranty and representation survival periods negotiated in the share purchase agreement.

### Who can act as an escrow agent under Turkish law?
Escrow agents can be reputable commercial banks, specialized financial institutions, or trusted law firms utilizing structured client account arrangements.

### What happens to escrow funds if a dispute arises between buyer and seller?
Disputed amounts remain blocked in the escrow account while undisputed portions are released, pending a final resolution through negotiation, arbitration, or court litigation as outlined in the SPA.
